Terrace Garden in Hyderabad: A Step-by-Step Guide

Grow your own vegetables, herbs and flowers on your rooftop — safely, and through every season.

By Mohammed Shoeib Ahmer, Founder of ShowBio Pvt. Ltd. · B.Sc. Horticulture · Published 12 November 2026

Step 1: Check the terrace first

Before adding a single pot, make sure the roof is ready. Check that the waterproofing is in good condition and that water drains freely to the outlets. Wet soil is heavy, so keep large planters near walls and columns, and ask a structural engineer before installing raised beds or heavy containers across the whole roof.

Step 2: Choose containers

  • Grow bags: light, affordable and breathable — great for vegetables.
  • Plastic or fibre pots: lightweight and long-lasting for herbs and flowers.
  • Raised beds on stands: keep moisture off the roof surface and are easier on the back.

Always place pots on stands or bricks so water does not sit on the waterproofing layer.

Step 3: Make a good potting mix

A simple, light mix that works well in Hyderabad is roughly one part garden soil, one part compost or vermicompost and one part cocopeat. Cocopeat keeps the mix light and holds water through hot afternoons; compost feeds the plants. Avoid using only red soil, which compacts and drains poorly in pots.

Step 4: Plan for sun and heat

Terraces get full sun, which is good for vegetables but harsh in April and May. Install a 35–50% green shade net over leafy vegetables and seedlings during peak summer, and remove it after the monsoon arrives.

Step 5: Pick plants by season

Monsoon (June–September)

  • Okra (bhindi), brinjal, chilli, tomato, gourds and beans
  • Flowers such as marigold and balsam

Winter (October–February)

  • Leafy greens: spinach (palak), fenugreek (methi), coriander and amaranth
  • Radish, carrot, beetroot, cabbage and cauliflower
  • Flowers such as petunia, dianthus and chrysanthemum

Summer (March–May)

  • Heat-tolerant crops such as gourds, cluster beans and amaranth, under a shade net
  • Hardy herbs: curry leaf, tulsi and lemongrass

Step 6: Water and feed smartly

  • Water early in the morning; in peak summer, small pots may need a second watering in the evening.
  • Mulch the top of each pot with dry leaves to reduce evaporation.
  • A simple drip kit with a timer saves water and time on larger terraces.
  • Feed every 2–3 weeks with compost tea, vermicompost or a diluted organic liquid fertiliser.

Step 7: Close the loop with composting

Kitchen waste such as vegetable peels and tea leaves can be composted in a bin on the terrace and returned to your pots — reducing waste and the cost of fertiliser.

Frequently asked questions

Is a terrace garden safe for my building?

Yes, if the waterproofing is in good condition, water drains freely and heavy planters are kept near walls and columns. For raised beds or many large containers, ask a structural engineer to confirm the roof load first.

What vegetables can I grow on a terrace in Hyderabad?

In the monsoon, grow okra, brinjal, chilli, tomato, gourds and beans. In winter, grow leafy greens, radish, carrot, beetroot, cabbage and cauliflower. In summer, grow heat-tolerant gourds and amaranth under a shade net.
